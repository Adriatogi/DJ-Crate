DJ Crate docs

Everything DJ Crate does, why it's built this way, and how it fits next to Rekordbox. These docs live inside the app, so they work offline.

What DJ Crate is

Your prep room before Rekordbox. DJ Crate helps you find songs, get the right audio files, catalogue them, and remember what works together. Then you take the music to Rekordbox to analyse, cue and perform.

Philosophy

  • The DJ decides. There is no recommendation algorithm, no "next song" suggestions and no auto-mixing, and there won't be. DJ Crate remembers what you found (your mixes, samples, notes and sets) and makes it easy to find again. It gives facts such as BPM, key and what samples what. It never picks for you.
  • Suggestions are never applied on their own. Genius samples, YouTube search results and genre suggestions from MusicBrainz or Rekordbox are always clicked, ticked or pasted by you. Your Genre Rack is the only list of genres.
  • One source for your library: Spotify. To add a song, add it to a Spotify playlist and import it. Songs don't live only inside DJ Crate.
  • You choose the audio. You paste the link. The length check helps you catch the wrong version, but it never swaps one in silently.
  • Local and yours. Everything is in your library folder on your Mac. No account, no cloud. Backups are automatic.
  • Rekordbox stays in charge of performance. DJ Crate never touches Rekordbox's database. The two only talk through files Rekordbox officially reads and writes.

DJ Crate and Rekordbox

DJ Crate is a layer above Rekordbox. They stay separate on purpose, with one line of communication going each way.

Rekordbox never pulls changes by itself. DJ Crate writes and updates its file by itself (you never make or edit it); you only import the playlists in Rekordbox. BPM, key, grids and cues are never sent from DJ Crate to Rekordbox, so nothing in Rekordbox can be overwritten with worse data. Songs are matched by file path, so you don't get duplicates. Set it up under Settings → Rekordbox.

Crates

  • Spotify import and re-sync. A playlist becomes a crate with every song's title, artists, album, cover and length. Songs removed on Spotify are kept, dimmed, with your notes and file. ↻ All next to the import button re-syncs every Spotify crate in one go. When importing, tick Hide already imported playlists to see only the new ones.
  • Smart crates fill themselves: Needs Link, Ready to Download, Downloaded, Ready, Broken, Check Version, Missing Files. You can also make your own crates and folders and drag songs into them.
  • Links. Paste a YouTube link, or use Find. It highlights the result whose length matches Spotify's and warns about Radio Edit, Live, Sped up and similar. It searches the next few songs ahead so Find opens instantly.
  • Downloads. Title - Artist.mp3, constant 192 kbps, tagged with title, artists, album, year, ISRC, and cover art. The comment tag is left alone.
  • Flags. Edited (you changed the audio on purpose), Ready (prepped), Broken (needs fixing; say what in the notes).
  • Check Version. A download more than 5 seconds off Spotify's length is probably a different version.
  • BPM & Key columns. Sortable, filled from your Rekordbox export. Key shows both notations, like 8A · Am.
  • The song card (click a cover, title or artist) holds notes, samples, metadata and file info. It is the same card everywhere in the app.
  • Player. ▶ previews a song under its row. Space plays and pauses.

Genre Rack

The Genre Rack tab is your own list of genres and sub-genres (e.g. House › Deep House). It is the only place genres come from, so “House”, “house” and “hip-hop / Hip Hop” can never become separate genres.

  • Tagging a song. The Genre column (and the song card) opens a dropdown of your rack. Type to search. If it isn't there, + Add “…” to the Genre Rack adds it on the spot, optionally inside another genre. A song can have several genres.
  • In the MP3. Genres are written into the file's Genre tag as House / Deep House / Amapiano, parent first, so Rekordbox shows them. For songs Rekordbox already has, use right-click → Reload Tag (to be confirmed). The comment tag is never touched.
  • The rack. Genres are cards. Click one to open it: its sub-genres show as cards, with its artists (from its songs; you never set an artist's genre) in a row and its songs below, in the same table as Crates. Drag a card onto another to nest it, drag songs onto a card to tag them, and use ⋯ for rename, other spellings (aliases), merge and delete.
  • Sub-genres. Open a genre and press + Sub-genre (or hover its name under My genres and click +). To make an existing genre a sub-genre of another, drag its card onto the other card, or use its ⋯ menu → Move inside another genre…; Move to the top level takes it back out.
  • Putting songs in a genre. Open it and press + Add songs, then click songs to add them (click again to undo; “Show” filters to songs with no genre, all songs, or the songs of any one genre), drag songs from Crates onto its card, or use the [+] square in a song's Genre column. Hover a genre on a song and click ✕ to remove it.
  • MusicBrainz genres. The left side lists only the genres MusicBrainz put your songs or their artists under that aren't in your rack yet (like “Pop”), with how many songs. + Add puts one in your rack, where you decide what it sits under; ✕ hides one you don't want. Searching looks through all ~2,200 names MusicBrainz has.
  • Other spellings. Give a genre aliases (“Hip-Hop/Rap” → add “Hip-Hop” and “Rap”) and names from Rekordbox, MP3s or MusicBrainz match it. Case and hyphens never matter.
  • Bring in genres… lists every genre name found in your Rekordbox XML and MP3s. Match each to your rack, ignore the ones you don't want, then apply to songs that have no genre yet.
  • ⚠ next to a genre means Rekordbox currently has a different genre for that song. Hover to see it. DJ Crate wins: Reload Tag in Rekordbox to update it.
  • Suggestions. A song with no genre shows faint chips in its Genre cell: the genre from Rekordbox or the MP3, and MusicBrainz suggestions looked up quietly in the background (dashed when it is the artist's genre, not necessarily the song's). Click a chip to use it, or the [+] square for the full dropdown. A ? means the name isn't in your rack yet: add it, make it an alias, or ignore it. Nothing is applied until you click.
  • Genre crates. Make crate turns a genre into a live crate that fills itself with its songs (and its sub-genres'). It shows in your sidebar with a 🏷 and goes to Rekordbox like your other crates. You can't drag songs in by hand; tag them instead.

Cutting

Every song has a board with the song in the middle. A mix is a note between songs, e.g. Shangri-La → [loop the last 8 bars, reverb, slow it down] → Elevators. Mixes are one-way. Steps 1 · 2 · 3 reveals the songs your song connects to, then theirs. Samples can be text or a link to another song in your library. Find samples asks Genius what a song samples or was sampled in, and you tick which to keep.

Set Setter

Build a set as a board of songs and free-text cards, wired together through mix notes. Branch as much as you like. The bright wires are your main path, the set as you'd play it. List shows it as a running order with start times and total length, and Plays for sets how long each song actually plays. Export as text copies it out. With Rekordbox sending turned on, each set becomes a playlist in main-path order.

A typical week

  1. Add songs to a Spotify playlist all week.
  2. Import or re-sync it in DJ Crate.
  3. Paste a link for each song (or use Find), then Download All.
  4. Bring the new files into Rekordbox, analyse them and set cues.
  5. Re-export your collection from Rekordbox, and BPM and key appear in DJ Crate.
  6. Note mixes and samples in Cutting as you practise.
  7. Plan the set in Set Setter.
  8. Send crates and sets to Rekordbox, import the playlists there and perform.

Backups and a new Mac

You need two separate backups: the library folder (every MP3 plus DJ Crate's data in .djcrate/), and Rekordbox's own backup (cues, grids, playlists, My Tags: File → Library → Backup Library). DJ Crate also saves its database every day, keeping the last 14, and Settings can export and import your crates.

To move to a new Mac: put the library folder at the same path, restore the Rekordbox backup, install DJ Crate and point it at the folder. Don't open the same library from two Macs at once.
