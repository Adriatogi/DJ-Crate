DJ Crate docs

Everything DJ Crate does, why it's built this way, and how it fits next to Rekordbox. These docs live inside the app, so they work offline.

What DJ Crate is

Your prep room before Rekordbox. DJ Crate helps you find songs, get the right audio files, catalogue them, and remember what works together. Then you take the music to Rekordbox to analyse, cue and perform.

Getting started

The first time takes about 10 minutes. The Getting started checklist (bottom right) follows along and ticks each step off; Settings → About → Setup guide brings it back.

  1. Open the app. Unzip the download and drag DJ Crate into Applications. The first time you open it, macOS says it can't verify the app (DJ Crate isn't paid-signed with Apple). Click Done, open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway, then enter your Mac password. You only do this once.
  2. Create a library. One folder for all your MP3s and DJ Crate's data (it's what Rekordbox imports). The app suggests ~/Music/DJ Crate; an external drive works too.
  3. Connect Spotify (once, about 2 minutes, needs Spotify Premium). Settings → Spotify walks you through it: make a free app on Spotify's developer dashboard, add the redirect URI (there's a Copy button), paste its Client ID and log in. (why your own app?) See the FAQ if it complains.
  4. Import a playlist. + Import Spotify playlist turns it into a crate with every song's title, artists, cover and length.
  5. Pick the links. In Needs Link, click Find ▾ on a song: the green result matches Spotify's length and has no "live", "remix" or "sped up" warnings. Or click Auto-fill links to fill every song that has a sure match and leave the rest for you.
  6. Download. Ready to Download → Download All. Each song becomes a tagged Title - Artist.mp3 in your library folder.
  7. Into Rekordbox (optional): drag the library folder into Rekordbox's Collection, and see the XML FAQ to bring BPM, key and My Tags back.

macOS may also ask once whether DJ Crate can use your Keychain (where your Spotify login is kept): enter your password and choose Always Allow.

Philosophy

  • The DJ decides. There is no recommendation algorithm, no "next song" suggestions and no auto-mixing, and there won't be. DJ Crate remembers what you found (your mixes, samples, notes and sets) and makes it easy to find again. It gives facts such as BPM, key and what samples what. It never picks for you.
  • Suggestions are never applied on their own. Genius samples and YouTube search results are always clicked, ticked or pasted by you.
  • One source for your library: Spotify. To add a song, add it to a Spotify playlist and import it. Songs don't live only inside DJ Crate.
  • You choose the audio. You paste the link. The length check helps you catch the wrong version, but it never swaps one in silently.
  • Local and yours. Everything is in your library folder on your Mac. No account, no cloud. Backups are automatic.
  • Rekordbox stays in charge of performance. DJ Crate never touches Rekordbox's database. The two only talk through files Rekordbox officially reads and writes.

DJ Crate and Rekordbox

DJ Crate is a layer above Rekordbox. They stay separate on purpose, with one line of communication going each way.

Rekordbox never pulls changes by itself. DJ Crate writes and updates its file by itself (you never make or edit it); you only import the playlists in Rekordbox. BPM, key, grids and cues are never sent from DJ Crate to Rekordbox, so nothing in Rekordbox can be overwritten with worse data. Songs are matched by file path, so you don't get duplicates. Set it up under Settings → Rekordbox.

My Tags stay Rekordbox's: DJ Crate can show them but never edits them. Writing to Rekordbox's own data can corrupt it, so they come in read-only through the XML export. Rekordbox only puts them there if you turn on its setting that adds My Tags to Comments (each song's Comments then starts like /* Hype / Vocal */). Then they show as a My Tags column, on each song's card, and in the My Tags filter button. To change a tag, change it in Rekordbox and export again. Editing that comment text elsewhere won't change the real My Tag.

Crates

  • Spotify import and re-sync. A playlist becomes a crate with every song's title, artists, album, cover and length. Songs removed on Spotify leave the crate; if they aren't in another crate they go to the Trash, with your notes and file, and come back if they return to the playlist. ↻ All next to the import button re-syncs every Spotify crate in one go. When importing, tick Hide already imported playlists to see only the new ones.
  • Smart crates fill themselves: Needs Link, Ready to Download, Downloaded, Check Version, Clean Versions, Missing Files, and Not in a Crate (songs in no crate at all, e.g. from a crate deleted before the Trash existed; Move all to Trash clears them out). You can also make your own crates and folders and drag songs into them.
  • Links. Paste a YouTube link, or use Find. It highlights the result whose length matches Spotify's and warns about Radio Edit, Live, Sped up and similar. It searches the next few songs ahead so Find opens instantly.
  • Auto-fill links. In any view (Needs Link, a crate, a folder), this fills in the link for each song without one from the same top 5 results Find shows, using one that's fully green: length within 3 seconds of Spotify's, the song's title and an artist in the result, and no version warnings (Remix, Live, Sped up, Clean, a live session…). When several are green it takes the official one: the artist's Topic channel, then their own channel, then an "Official Audio/Video" upload, then anything else. Songs without one stay for you to pick. Undo auto-fill takes back the Auto links that aren't downloaded yet, so those songs go back to Needs Link. It never replaces a link you set and never downloads: filled links get an Auto marker until you download or change them, so give them a quick listen and hit Download All. It searches each song fresh, one at a time (so running it again looks again), and pauses if YouTube rate-limits.
  • Download progress. While songs download, a bar at the top shows 2/3 downloaded and fills up as each song finishes (hover to see which songs are downloading). A failed song shows as · 1 failed in red; click to see why and retry. When the run ends it says how many downloaded or failed. Click it to open Activity.
  • Downloads. Title - Artist.mp3, constant 192 kbps, tagged with title, artists, album, year, ISRC, and cover art. The comment tag is left alone.
  • Check Version. A download more than 5 seconds off Spotify's length is probably a different version.
  • BPM & Key columns. Sortable, filled from your Rekordbox export. Key shows both notations, like 8A · Am.
  • The song card (click a cover, title or artist) holds notes, samples, metadata and file info. It is the same card everywhere in the app.
  • Player. ▶ previews a song under its row. Space plays and pauses.
  • Trash. In the sidebar, below the smart crates. Songs land here when you Move to Trash, when you delete a crate (its songs that aren't in another crate), and when they're removed from a Spotify playlist (same rule). They don't count anywhere else: not in All Tracks, Needs Link, downloads or Rekordbox. Each shows why it's there. Restore brings a song back with its notes, file and crates. Empty Trash (or Delete permanently) asks first, then deletes for good; MP3s go to the macOS Trash.
  • Hidden. Next to Trash in the sidebar. Tick any songs and choose Hide to keep them out of every list and count without deleting anything, e.g. songs whose files are gone for good. Unhide brings them back.

Clean and explicit versions

YouTube often serves the clean (radio) edit of a song without saying so in the title. YouTube Music lists many albums twice: once marked explicit and once not. DJ Crate checks each song's YouTube link in the background, so it knows both copies and which one you have. Some gigs want clean, so it never decides for you.

  • Clean (a grey label) next to a title means the link is the clean copy, and YouTube Music also has the explicit one. Hover it to see which. Explicit songs show nothing.
  • Swapping. Click the label → Swap to the explicit version. For an explicit song that also has a clean copy, the song card offers Swap to clean. Either way the song downloads again to the same file, so Rekordbox keeps its link (check your cues and beatgrid). The link and label only switch once the new file is in: if the download fails, the song keeps its old file and label and shows in Ready to Download (“Explicit not downloaded”), where Download tries again. You can swap back any time.
  • Clean Versions in the smart crates lists every clean song, with Swap all to explicit. Select songs to get Swap to explicit or Swap to clean in the bar at the bottom.
  • Find notes which results are the clean copy, with a link to use the explicit one instead.
  • What it can't tell. Only official YouTube Music tracks are marked explicit or not, so user uploads and music videos get no label (their song card offers the official explicit copy if there is one). Listening to the audio alone can't reliably tell a clean edit (a few muted words look like ordinary pauses), and re-recorded radio lines can't be detected at all, so give a new song a listen anyway.
  • Turn it off under Settings → Clean versions.

Cutting

Every song has a board with the song in the middle. A mix is a note between songs, e.g. Shangri-La → [loop the last 8 bars, reverb, slow it down] → Elevators. Mixes are one-way. Steps 1 · 2 · 3 reveals the songs your song connects to, then theirs. Samples can be text or a link to another song in your library. Find samples asks Genius what a song samples or was sampled in, and you tick which to keep.

Set Setter

Build a set as a board of songs and free-text cards, wired together through mix notes. Branch as much as you like. The bright wires are your main path, the set as you'd play it. List shows it as a running order with start times and total length, and Plays for sets how long each song actually plays. Export as text copies it out. With Rekordbox sending turned on, each set becomes a playlist in main-path order.

A typical week

  1. Add songs to a Spotify playlist all week.
  2. Import or re-sync it in DJ Crate.
  3. Paste a link for each song (or use Find), then Download All.
  4. Bring the new files into Rekordbox, analyse them and set cues.
  5. Re-export your collection from Rekordbox, and BPM and key appear in DJ Crate.
  6. Note mixes and samples in Cutting as you practise.
  7. Plan the set in Set Setter.
  8. Send crates and sets to Rekordbox, import the playlists there and perform.

FAQ

How do I update the Rekordbox XML?

DJ Crate reads BPM, key and My Tags from an XML file that Rekordbox writes. Rekordbox never updates that file by itself, so export again whenever you change something there.

  1. In DJ Crate, open Settings → Rekordbox and note the file path shown. The first time, export first (step 2) and then click Choose XML file….
  2. In Rekordbox, choose File → Export Collection in xml format from the menu bar.
  3. Save it over that same file (same folder, same name) and click Replace.
  4. DJ Crate picks it up within about 10 seconds, or click Import now. The Settings line should say “updated just now”.

If saving over the old file is awkward, save it anywhere and use Choose a different file… in Settings.

How do I see my My Tags in DJ Crate?

  1. In Rekordbox's Preferences, turn on the setting that adds My Tags to Comments. Each song's Comments then start with its tags, like /* Hype / Vocal */.
  2. Export the XML again (above).

A My Tags column appears in the song list, the My Tags ▾ button filters by a tag, and each song's card lists them. They're read-only: DJ Crate never writes My Tags or Comments, because writing to Rekordbox's data can corrupt it. To change a tag, change it in Rekordbox and export again.

Why do fewer songs match than I have in Rekordbox?

Songs are matched by file path. Only songs whose MP3 is in your DJ Crate library folder match, so songs Rekordbox has from other folders or drives don't count. If the number looks far too low, the XML is probably old: export it again.

How do my crates and sets get into Rekordbox?

Turn on Send my crates & sets to Rekordbox in Settings → Rekordbox. DJ Crate then keeps DJ Crate for Rekordbox.xml in your library folder up to date by itself. One-time setup in Rekordbox:

  1. Preferences → Advanced → Database → rekordbox xml → Imported Library: choose that file.
  2. Preferences → View → Layout: tick “rekordbox xml” so it shows in the sidebar.
  3. In that section, right-click a playlist → Import Playlist. Do it again after changing a crate.

Crates become playlists and sets become playlists in main-path order. Only playlists are sent: never BPM, key, grids, cues, notes or tags, so nothing in Rekordbox can be overwritten. Songs are matched by file path, so there are no duplicates.

Spotify setup says "Invalid redirect URI" or "not on the user list"

  • INVALID_CLIENT: Invalid redirect URI: in your app on developer.spotify.com/dashboard, add exactly http://127.0.0.1:8765/callback (Settings → Spotify has a Copy button), save, and log in again.
  • Not on this app's user list: someone else's Spotify developer app is being used. In that dashboard, open the app → User Management and add your Spotify email, or make your own app.
  • Spotify only lets apps read playlists you own or collaborate on, plus Liked Songs. For anyone else's, copy its songs into a new playlist of your own.

Why do I have to make my own Spotify app?

DJ Crate reads your playlists through Spotify's official Web API, and every app using it needs a Client ID. A shared one would be easier, but Spotify only lets a hobby app's creator approve about 25 people by hand (by email), and opening an app to everyone is only offered to registered companies with a large user base. So each DJ makes their own free app instead.

  • It takes about 2 minutes, once (Settings → Spotify, or the Getting started guide).
  • It's just for you: your Client ID and login stay on your Mac (the login is in the macOS Keychain).
  • DJ Crate only reads playlists: names, artists, albums, lengths and covers. It never changes anything in your Spotify account.

YouTube says "Sign in to confirm you're not a bot". What do cookies do?

Some videos (age-restricted ones, or when YouTube gets suspicious) only download when you're signed in. Settings → Downloads → Use cookies from browser lets DJ Crate use the YouTube sign-in from a browser on this Mac.

  1. Sign in to youtube.com in that browser (Chrome, Safari, Firefox, Brave, Edge…) and play any video once.
  2. Pick the browser in Settings. If it has several profiles, pick the one signed in to YouTube. DJ Crate tests it right away.
  3. Chrome, Brave, Edge: macOS asks whether DJ Crate may use "Chrome Safe Storage". Enter your Mac password and choose Always Allow (choosing Deny is the usual reason cookies "don't work").
  4. Safari: needs Full Disk Access. System Settings → Privacy & Security → Full Disk Access → turn on DJ Crate.

DJ Crate keeps only YouTube and Google cookies, in a private file on this Mac, and reads them again every few hours. Turning cookies off deletes that file.

Backups and a new Mac

You need two separate backups: the library folder (every MP3 plus DJ Crate's data in .djcrate/), and Rekordbox's own backup (cues, grids, playlists, My Tags: File → Library → Backup Library). DJ Crate also saves its database every day, keeping the last 14, and Settings can export and import your crates.

To move to a new Mac: put the library folder at the same path, restore the Rekordbox backup, install DJ Crate and point it at the folder. Don't open the same library from two Macs at once.
